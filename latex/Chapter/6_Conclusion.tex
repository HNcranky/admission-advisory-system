\section{Conclusion}

This thesis set out to close three gaps that existing Vietnamese admission-information channels leave open: contradictory official data that no tool reconciles, the absence of grounded yet personalized advice, and the inaccessibility of heterogeneous and scanned source formats.
The result is an end-to-end system that ingests official sources into a canonical, conflict-aware store and serves a Vietnamese-language advisory chat over that store.

Measured against the requirements derived in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Related_works}, the system achieves its core goals.
It collects a student profile incrementally and withholds recommendations until the critical information is present.
It answers factual questions only from ingested documents, with citations, and declines honestly when the corpus is insufficient, rather than fabricating figures as a general-purpose chatbot would.
It preserves contradictory source values rather than overwriting them, and it discloses the disagreement in its recommendations.
It stays available through model outages by routing every model call through a gateway that distinguishes hard from soft failures and falls back deterministically.
These behaviors are backed by a substantial, well-tested implementation --- roughly 28{,}000 lines of Python, 856 automated tests with 854 passing, and a populated data store drawn from real official sources --- as quantified in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Experiment}.
Relative to the channels surveyed in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Related_works}, the distinguishing achievement is conflict awareness: where aggregator portals and institutional platforms silently pick one value, this system surfaces the conflict and tells the student to verify it.

The system also has clear limits.
Of the 25 conversational edge cases used for evaluation, four are only partially handled and four are not yet met.
These eight cases concentrate on one weakness: location and tuition preferences are stored as flat strings and are not consumed by the retrieval and reasoning stages, so a student's stated region or budget does not yet shape or expand the search the way it should.
The configured data also covers only a few universities, so the system is a working demonstration on real data rather than a comprehensive national service.

\section{Future work}
\label{section:6.2}

The most valuable next step follows directly from the evaluation.
Replacing the flat location and tuition fields with structured soft preferences, and threading them through retrieval and reasoning, would close the cluster of unmet edge cases (EC-07, EC-08, EC-11, EC-19, EC-20, and EC-25) in a single coherent change: budget would filter and rank candidates explicitly, a stated region would constrain the search and a relaxed one would expand it, and a removed major would be dropped from the profile.

A second direction is breadth of coverage.
Because the ingestion pipeline is configuration-driven, adding a university is largely a matter of providing its source configuration and normalization dictionaries; extending coverage toward the full set of major Vietnamese universities would turn the demonstration into a generally useful tool.

A third direction is production hardening.
The current deployment is a single-node Docker Compose setup suitable for development and demonstration; a production service would need authenticated operator access, scheduled re-ingestion to keep data fresh through the admission season, monitoring of the inference quota and fallback rates, and load testing of the background-execution model under concurrent students.

Taken together, these directions would extend a system that already demonstrates conflict-aware, grounded admission advising on real data into one that could serve Vietnamese students broadly and reliably.
