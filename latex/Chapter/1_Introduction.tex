\section{Motivation}
\label{section:1.1}

Choosing a university program is one of the most consequential decisions a Vietnamese student makes, and it must be made from information that is unusually hard to assemble.
The relevant facts --- which programs admit which subject combinations, how many places each program offers, what last year's cutoff scores were, and which admission methods apply --- are scattered across three kinds of sources that do not share a format.
Each university publishes its own admission pages, structured differently from every other university's.
Detailed admission proposals are released as \gls{PDF} documents, some of which are scanned images rather than digital text.
Nationwide rules are issued separately by the \gls{MOET}, which also operates the central application portal.

This information is not only fragmented but unstable and inconsistent.
Admission documents are revised repeatedly through an admission season, so a figure read in one week may be superseded the next.
Worse, sources disagree: two official publications for the same program can state different quotas, and an aggregator portal can republish a cutoff score that no longer matches the school's own page.
A student facing these contradictions has no straightforward way to tell which value is current or authoritative.
Because an application decision is hard to reverse and the competition for places is intense, acting on a stale or incorrect figure carries a real cost.
The motivation for this thesis is the gap between the importance of the decision and the poor state of the information available to make it.

\section{Objectives and scope of the graduation thesis}
\label{section:1.2}

Students currently bridge this gap with channels that each fall short.
Official school pages and ministry documents are authoritative but fragmented and unreconciled.
Hotlines and human counselors personalize their advice but cannot scale to every student at peak season.
Aggregator portals improve discoverability but strip away provenance, so a student cannot judge a republished number.
General-purpose \gls{LLM} chatbots are conversational and always available, but their training data lags the admission season and they fabricate specific figures when pressed.
Specialized admission-engagement platforms used by universities abroad show that grounded, knowledge-base-backed admission chat is practical, but they are built around a single institution's own trusted data and are not designed to reconcile contradictory figures from independent sources.
A fuller comparison of these channels is given in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Related_works}.

Across all of them, three gaps recur: none reconciles contradictory official data, none combines grounded answers with incremental personalization, and none collects a student profile gradually and withholds recommendations until it has enough data to give them responsibly.

The objective of this thesis is to build an end-to-end system that closes these gaps for the Vietnamese admission context.
Concretely, the system has three parts: an ingestion pipeline that collects and normalizes data from official sources, a canonical and conflict-aware data store that preserves rather than discards contradictory values, and an \gls{LLM}-based advisory chat that collects a student profile over a conversation and produces grounded, conflict-aware recommendations.
The scope is deliberately bounded.
The configured data sources are the universities registered in the ingestion configuration --- principally HUST and VNU-UET for structured admission data, with HUST, NEU, VNU-UET, and national \gls{MOET} documents in the knowledge corpus --- rather than every Vietnamese university.
The user-facing dialogue is conducted in Vietnamese, the language of its intended students, while this thesis is written in English.
Training or fine-tuning a language model is out of scope; the work concerns engineering a reliable system around a hosted model.

\section{Tentative solution}
\label{section:1.3}

The system is built from three technologies matched to the three gaps.
Grounded answering is provided by retrieval-augmented generation over a vector store, so factual replies are conditioned on ingested official documents and cite them.
The advisory consultation is orchestrated as a fixed LangGraph pipeline whose six stages --- profile, retrieve, conflict, reason, policy, explain --- run in a deterministic, traceable order.
All model access passes through a single resilient inference gateway that distinguishes hard \gls{API} failures from malformed-output failures and degrades gracefully through retries, fallbacks, and deterministic alternatives.

The thesis makes three principal contributions, developed in Chapter~\ref{chapter:SolutionAndContribution}.
The first is conflict-aware data consolidation: contradictory quota and cutoff figures are preserved per source and surfaced in recommendations, with model judgment confined to the narrow cases where it is safe.
The second is the resilient inference gateway, which keeps the assistant usable through model outages by treating the two failure modes differently and providing a deterministic fallback for intent routing.
The third is end-to-end ingestion of heterogeneous official sources, including a hybrid text-and-\gls{OCR} extractor with an explicit safeguard against model transcription failures on scanned documents.
The resulting system comprises roughly 28{,}000 lines of Python verified by 856 automated tests, with a populated canonical store and knowledge corpus drawn from real official sources, as quantified in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Experiment}.

\section{Thesis organization}
\label{section:1.4}

The remainder of this thesis is organized into five chapters.
Chapter~\ref{chapter:Related_works} surveys the admission-information channels students rely on today, identifies their shared limitations, and from that analysis derives the system's functional and non-functional requirements as a use-case model.
Chapter~\ref{chapter:Methodology} reviews the theoretical background and the technologies selected to meet those requirements, presenting each technology together with the requirement it serves and the alternative that was rejected.
Chapter~\ref{chapter:Experiment} presents the design, implementation, and evaluation of the system: its layered architecture, the design of its core packages, the user interface, the database schema, and the results of testing it against an automated suite and a catalogue of conversational edge cases.
Chapter~\ref{chapter:SolutionAndContribution} steps back from the full system to examine its three principal contributions in depth, each framed as a problem, a solution, and a result.
Chapter~\ref{chapter:conclusion} concludes by assessing what was and was not achieved relative to existing products and outlines directions for future work.
