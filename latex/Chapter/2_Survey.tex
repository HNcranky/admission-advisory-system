Chapter~\ref{chapter:Introduction} introduced the fragmented state of Vietnamese university admission information and outlined the goal of building a conflict-aware advisory assistant.
This chapter grounds that goal in concrete requirements.
Section~\ref{section:2.1} surveys the channels and tools that prospective students currently rely on and identifies their shared limitations.
Sections~\ref{section:2.2} and~\ref{section:2.3} translate those limitations into a use-case model and detailed functional specifications.
Section~\ref{section:2.4} closes with the non-functional requirements that shape the architecture presented in later chapters.

\section{Status survey}
\label{section:2.1}

Vietnamese students preparing for university admission currently gather information through five kinds of channels.

The first channel is the set of \textit{official publication sources}: each university maintains its own admission pages and publishes annual proposal documents, while the \gls{MOET} issues nationwide regulations and operates the national application portal.
These sources are authoritative, but they are fragmented by design.
A student comparing three universities must locate and read three differently structured websites and several \gls{PDF} documents, some of which are scanned images rather than digital text.
The documents are also revised repeatedly during an admission season, and different publications from the same period can contradict each other on quotas, cutoff scores, or admission methods.

The second channel is \textit{human counseling}: school-run hotlines, admission fairs, and teacher advice.
Human counselors can personalize their guidance, but their availability is limited to office hours and peak-season capacity, and the answers depend on the individual counselor's familiarity with the latest regulations.

The third channel is \textit{aggregator portals} that compile cutoff scores and program lists from many universities into searchable tables.
These portals improve discoverability, but they republish data without provenance: when an aggregator's number disagrees with the school's own page, the student has no way to tell which value is current.

The fourth channel is \textit{general-purpose \gls{LLM} chatbots}.
They are conversational and always available, but their training data lags the admission season by months or years, and they fabricate plausible-sounding numbers when asked about specific quotas or cutoffs.
Without grounding in current official documents, their answers cannot be trusted for a decision as consequential as a university application.

The fifth channel is \textit{specialized admission engagement platforms} deployed by universities abroad, such as Element451's AI-first student engagement suite~\cite{element451} and the Mainstay platform~\cite{mainstay}, built on conversational frameworks of the kind exemplified by Rasa's \gls{CALM} architecture~\cite{rasa_calm} and Google Dialogflow CX~\cite{dialogflow}.
These products demonstrate that grounded, knowledge-base-backed admission chat is practical at scale.
However, they are institution-centric: each deployment serves one university's own funnel, presumes a single trusted data source maintained by that institution, and is not designed to reconcile contradictory figures published by independent Vietnamese sources.

Table~\ref{table:channel-comparison} summarizes the comparison.

These channels can be situated against two strands of prior work.
Research on \gls{LLM} reliability documents that models without access to current, authoritative context hallucinate factual details, producing fluent but unfaithful text~\cite{ji2023hallucination}; this is the failure mode that makes a general-purpose chatbot unsafe for quoting a specific quota or cutoff.
It is also why the commercial education platforms cited above --- Element451~\cite{element451} and Mainstay~\cite{mainstay}, built on dialogue frameworks such as Rasa's \gls{CALM} architecture~\cite{rasa_calm} and Google Dialogflow CX~\cite{dialogflow} --- pair conversation with a grounding knowledge base rather than relying on a model's parametric memory.
What none of this prior work targets is a setting in which the grounding sources are themselves mutually inconsistent: those systems assume one institution curates one trusted corpus, whereas a Vietnamese applicant must reconcile several independent official publications that openly disagree.

Three gaps recur across every channel.
First, no channel reconciles contradictory data: even authoritative sources disagree with each other, and every existing tool silently picks one value or presents none.
In the Vietnamese context this is not an edge case --- quotas, cutoff scores, and admission methods are revised repeatedly within a single season --- so a tool that commits to one value is frequently wrong without signalling it.
Second, no channel combines personalization with grounding: human counselors personalize but do not scale, while portals scale but treat every student identically.
Aggregators and generic chatbots reach any applicant, but neither weighs an individual's score, subject combination, and budget against a specific program's published requirements.
Third, no conversational tool collects a student profile incrementally and gates its recommendations on having sufficient data, the way a careful human counselor does.
A student who states only a vague interest such as ``thích lập trình'' cannot be served a reliable recommendation until enough of a profile exists to constrain retrieval to admissible programs.
The system described in this thesis is designed to close these three gaps for the Vietnamese admission context.

\section{Functional overview}
\label{section:2.2}

\subsection{General use case diagram}
\label{subsection:2.2.1}

The system serves two actors.
The \textit{student} interacts anonymously with the chat interface: no account or personal identifier is required, and a session is addressed only by a random token.
The \textit{operator} maintains the system: they run the data-ingestion \gls{CLI} against configured sources and inspect the per-stage trace panel when diagnosing an advisory run.
Figure~\ref{fig:usecase-general} shows the general use case diagram.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[height=9cm]{usecase_general.png}
    \caption{General use case diagram of the admission advisory system}
    \label{fig:usecase-general}
\end{figure}

The student's use cases correspond to the conversation routes the system distinguishes: a full advisory consultation, a factual knowledge question, a hybrid comparison that needs both, and profile control actions such as resetting the collected profile.
The operator's use cases cover the ingestion lifecycle (running ingestion per school, per source, or for an ad-hoc \gls{URL}) and observability (inspecting the stage-by-stage trace of an advisory run).

\subsection{Detailed use case diagrams}
\label{subsection:2.2.2}

\subsubsection{Advisory consultation}

Figure~\ref{fig:usecase-advisory} decomposes the advisory consultation.
The consultation is a multi-turn dialogue: the system asks for missing profile information one slot at a time, and the student may answer the pending question, volunteer several values in one message, correct an earlier value, or abandon the profile and start over.
The consultation ends with ranked program recommendations that include explicit notes about conflicting source data.

\subsubsection{Knowledge question answering and hybrid comparison}

Figure~\ref{fig:usecase-knowledge} decomposes factual question answering.
A student may ask a school- or topic-scoped factual question (tuition, curriculum, scholarships, dormitories, careers, admission policy, or a program overview) and receives an answer grounded in ingested official documents, together with citations of the sources used.
When the retrieved material is not relevant enough, the system answers honestly that it has no data instead of fabricating a response.
A hybrid comparison combines this factual lookup with advisory data, for example when a student asks to compare two universities on both cutoff scores and tuition.

\subsubsection{Data ingestion}

Figure~\ref{fig:usecase-ingestion} decomposes the operator-facing ingestion workflow.
The operator can list the registered schools, ingest all sources of one school, re-run a single registered source, or ingest an ad-hoc \gls{URL}; every variant ends by persisting normalized records into the canonical store.
A separate ingestion path loads unstructured documents (tuition pages, curriculum \gls{PDF}s, scanned regulation documents) into the knowledge corpus that backs factual question answering.

\subsection{Business process}
\label{subsection:2.2.3}

Figure~\ref{fig:activity-advisory} shows the end-to-end business process triggered by every student message.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[height=19cm]{activity_advisory_process.png}
    \caption{Business process for handling a student message}
    \label{fig:activity-advisory}
\end{figure}

The process applies deterministic checks before any \gls{LLM} call.
Profile information contained in the message is extracted first, so a message like ``Em thi A01, với muốn học ở Hà Nội'' updates two profile fields at once regardless of which question was pending.
Explicit control actions (resetting the profile, rejecting a suggested value) and bare answers to the pending slot question are also handled deterministically.
Only when none of these shortcuts apply is the message classified by the intent router into one of seven routes: advisory flow, knowledge question, hybrid comparison, conversational small talk, clarification, out-of-scope, or profile reset.

An advisory-flow message proceeds only when all critical profile slots are filled; otherwise the system asks the next missing question.
Once the profile is sufficient, the run is dispatched to a background executor that drives the six-stage advisory pipeline --- profile, retrieve, conflict, reason, policy, explain --- and persists the final answer together with a per-stage trace.
The chat interface polls for the result and renders both the recommendation and, for the operator, the stage trace.

\section{Functional description}
\label{section:2.3}

This section specifies the four central use cases.
Extended specifications, including the full slot-by-slot dialogue obligations derived from the system's acceptance scenarios, are collected in Appendix~A.

\subsection{UC-01: Advisory consultation}

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \caption{Specification of UC-01 --- Advisory consultation}
    \label{table:uc01}
    \begin{tabular}{|p{3.2cm}|p{10.3cm}|}
        \hline
        \textbf{Actor} & Student \\
        \hline
        \textbf{Trigger} & The student asks for program or school advice. \\
        \hline
        \textbf{Pre-conditions} & An anonymous chat session exists. \\
        \hline
        \textbf{Post-conditions} & The session stores ranked recommendations, the collected profile, and a per-stage trace of the advisory run. \\
        \hline
        \textbf{Main flow} &
        1. The student expresses an advisory need, possibly vaguely (``thích lập trình với AI''). \newline
        2. The system extracts any profile values already present in the message. \newline
        3. The system asks for missing critical slots one at a time: admission year, total score, admission method, preferred majors, and subject combination. \newline
        4. When all critical slots are filled, the system starts a background advisory run. \newline
        5. The pipeline retrieves matching programs, checks source conflicts, ranks candidates, applies regulation policies, and generates an explained answer. \newline
        6. The student receives ranked recommendations with reasons and conflict warnings. \\
        \hline
        \textbf{Alternative flows} &
        A1. The student answers a different slot than asked: the provided values are stored and the pending question is repeated only if still unanswered. \newline
        A2. The student corrects an earlier value (``chỉ khoảng 25.75 thôi''): the profile is updated and the advisory run is re-executed with a note explaining why the ranking changed. \newline
        A3. The student asks to start over: the profile is cleared and collection restarts (UC-04). \newline
        A4. No program satisfies all constraints: the system states which criteria failed and suggests which constraint to relax. \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Two of the critical slots deserve comment.
The system distinguishes \textit{explicitly chosen majors} from \textit{interest tags inferred} from phrases such as ``thích lập trình'': inferred interests satisfy the major slot for retrieval purposes but are never treated as a confirmed program choice.
The optional slots --- location preference and tuition budget --- are collected when volunteered but do not block the consultation.

\section{Non-functional requirement}
\label{section:2.4}

The functional behavior above is constrained by six non-functional requirements.

\textbf{NFR-01 --- Graceful degradation under \gls{LLM} outage.}
Every \gls{LLM}-backed step must have a deterministic fallback, because the assistant depends on an external inference service with quotas and transient failures.
Intent classification falls back to keyword rules, knowledge answering degrades to an honest ``no data'' reply, and structured-output failures are retried or routed to fallback models rather than surfaced as crashes.
An advisory consultation must never silently stall because one classification call failed.

\textbf{NFR-02 --- Conflict tolerance and data freshness.}
The store must preserve disagreeing values rather than overwrite them: canonical records are unique per source, so two official publications quoting different quotas coexist as two rows with provenance.
Recommendations built on conflicting data must disclose the conflict and recommend checking the latest official announcement.
Ingestion must be re-runnable at any time, with idempotent migrations and upsert semantics, so the operator can refresh a school during the admission season.

\textbf{NFR-03 --- Anonymity.}
Students must be able to use the assistant without registering: sessions are identified only by a random token, and the collected profile contains academic attributes (scores, preferred majors) rather than personal identifiers.

\textbf{NFR-04 --- Responsiveness.}
A full advisory run involves several \gls{LLM} calls and database queries and may take tens of seconds.
The message endpoint must therefore acknowledge immediately and execute the run on a background executor, while the interface polls for completion and shows per-stage progress instead of a frozen screen.

\textbf{NFR-05 --- Testability.}
The test suite must run against an isolated database that is created automatically and never touches development data, so that integration tests can exercise real \gls{SQL} paths safely.
Repositories must accept injectable connection factories so tests can substitute fixtures.

\textbf{NFR-06 --- Crawling resilience.}
Several official Vietnamese sources, including government domains, serve broken \gls{TLS} certificate chains.
The fetcher must be able to proceed with verification disabled by explicit configuration, and every such fetch must be logged so the relaxation is visible rather than silent.

This chapter derived the system's requirements from a survey of existing admission-information channels.
The survey showed that no current channel reconciles contradictory official data, and none combines grounded answers with incremental, profile-aware personalization.
The resulting requirements were specified as a use-case model centered on an anonymous multi-turn advisory consultation, grounded knowledge answering, and operator-driven ingestion, constrained by graceful degradation, conflict tolerance, anonymity, responsiveness, testability, and crawling resilience.
Chapter~\ref{chapter:Methodology} reviews the theoretical background and the technologies selected to satisfy these requirements.
